## 1) The supply number everyone quotes is not the supply you can buy

XCP's live supply is about **2.59M**, net of fee burns. But the market behaves like a **low-float asset** because a large portion is either:

- **Trapped** (defunct/delisted exchanges)
- **Likely lost** (never moved since the 2014 burn)
- **Sleeping** (multi-year non-selling / non-outbound behavior)
- **Locked** (protocol escrow)

| General holders | 1,747,088 | 67.5% | Circulating (but mostly dormant) |

**Key claim:** ~24% of supply (~629K XCP) is effectively not coming back to market under normal conditions. The **real circulating supply** is closer to **~1.92M XCP** (general holders + active CEX) *before* you even account for dormancy / non-selling behavior.

## 2) Dormancy: most of the supply is not participating in price discovery

The original "active" numbers most people cite are based on **any ledger activity** (credits or debits). That can be misleading because:

- Exchanges can look "active" due to internal shuffles
- An address can look "active" by only receiving (no sell-side intent)

So here's the corrected approach.

### Hybrid dormancy (corrected)

**Definition:**

- If an address has **ever debited** XCP: dormancy = time since **last debit** (sell-side proxy)
- If an address has **never debited** XCP: dormancy = time since **first credit** (how long they've been holding)

This is the cleanest "how long has this balance sat without sell-side behavior" approximation you can compute without true coin-age tracking.

| Within 30 days | 102 | 118,005 | 4.6% |
| 31 days – 1 year | 587 | 221,698 | 8.7% |

| 3–5 years | 2,564 | 333,984 | 13.1% |
| **5+ years** | **11,908** | **1,498,411** | **58.8%** |

### The float that actually matters

Two "active" numbers matter, depending on how strict you want to be:

**Recently acquired or spent (<1y, hybrid):**
**~340K XCP (13.3%) across 689 addresses**

This includes:
- Addresses that spent/sold recently (debits), and
- Addresses that received recently and haven't sold yet (new holders)

**Sell-side active (<1y, debit-based):**
**~196K XCP (7.7%) across 452 addresses**

This is the cleanest proxy for "how much supply has demonstrated selling/spending behavior recently."

**Key claim:** XCP trades like an asset with a **thin sell-side float** sitting on top of a **very large dormant base**. That combination is exactly how you get violent repricings: a thin marginal market with big narrative surface area.

## 4) The whale alarm: Supply-Years Destroyed (SYD)

Most people use velocity as a proxy for activity. But velocity can be fake—same coins churning.

A better structural signal is **SYD (Supply-Years Destroyed)**: a proxy for whether *old supply is waking up*.

**The formula:** `SYD = LTDD ÷ (Supply × 365)`

Where LTDD sums `amount × days_since_last_activity` at spend time (address-level "last touch" proxy).

### How to read SYD

| Pattern | What It Means |
|---------|---------------|
| High velocity + low SYD | Churn/speculation (same coins moving) |
| High velocity + high SYD | Genuine distribution (old coins moving) |
| Low velocity + low SYD | Quiet accumulation (little disturbance) |

### The historical record

| Year | Price Δ | Velocity | SYD | What Happened |
|------|--------:|---------:|----:|---------------|
| 2014 | -85% | 2.57× | 0.36 | Post-burn capitulation |
| 2015 | +172% | 1.25× | 0.16 | Quiet rally |
| 2016 | **+1584%** | 2.72× | **0.81** | Distribution into strength |
| 2017 | -94% | 3.83× | **0.60** | Distribution into weakness |
| 2018 | -35% | 2.16× | 0.21 | Consolidation |
| 2019 | -28% | 0.97× | 0.04 | Quiet |
| 2020 | **+647%** | 0.25× | 0.05 | Organic rally |
| 2021 | -77% | 2.46× | 0.14 | Churn (same coins) |
| 2022 | +108% | 1.02× | 0.09 | Recovery |
| 2023 | +74% | 0.63× | 0.04 | Accumulation |
| 2024 | -76% | 0.39× | 0.06 | Capitulation |
| 2025 | +33% | 0.33× | 0.06 | Consolidation |

**2016 (SYD = 0.81):** Early holders distributed into strong ICO-boom demand. Price 16x'd.

**2017 (SYD = 0.60):** Distribution continued, but demand dried up. Price collapsed 94%.

**2021 (Velocity = 2.46×, SYD = 0.14):** The "fake" activity. Same coins trading rapidly during NFT hype. No structural change. Price fell 77%.

**2020 (Velocity = 0.25×, SYD = 0.05):** The healthiest pattern. Quiet accumulation, dormant supply undisturbed. Then price discovery: +647%.

**Key claim:** SYD doesn't predict direction. It predicts structural change. High SYD means old supply is moving—whether into strength or weakness depends on demand.

I am claiming the on-chain structure is consistent with a low-float base. If demand returns while dormant supply stays dormant, there's not much float to absorb it.

## 6) What would break this thesis

If you want to be intellectually honest about "low float," you need to name the failure modes.

**1. Dormant supply wakes up**

A true supply crunch ends the moment old holders decide "this is my exit." Watch SYD and last-debit shifts.

**2. Trapped balances re-enter circulation**

Unlikely without a withdrawal path, but not impossible (legal/bankruptcy outcomes, etc.).

**3. New exchange listings expand the accessible float**

More venues = more liquidity = less convexity.

**4. Protocol activity stays low forever**

Low volume can mean exhaustion, or it can mean irrelevance. The difference is whether Counterparty activity returns.

## Appendix: Methodology

**Data source:** Full Counterparty event history (19.9M events), blocks 278,000–933,605 (Jan 2014–Jan 2025).

**Reconciliation:** Supply reconstructed from ledger entries matches protocol supply within **2 XCP**.

**Volume definitions:**
- *Transfer Volume:* XCP debits for `send`, `mpma send`, `sweep`
- *Settlement Volume:* XCP credits for `order match`, `dispense`, `btcpay`
- *Economic Volume:* Transfer + Settlement (used for velocity)

**Dormancy (hybrid):**
- If address has ever debited XCP: **last debit date**
- If address has never debited XCP: **first credit date**

**SYD calculation:** Uses address-level "last-touch" proxy (LTDD). Not perfect coin-age (would require FIFO/LIFO tracking). May overstate age for mixed-age holdings.

**Exchange tagging:** Conservative estimates based on known/identified wallets. "Trapped" figures are lower bounds.
