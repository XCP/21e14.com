Because the market doesn't trade the full ledger supply. It trades the **float**—the set of coins that can plausibly show up as sell-side liquidity when bids appear.

**XCP's price-setting float is tiny.**

## TL;DR

Most people repeat “XCP has 2.59M supply” and stop there. That number is real, but it’s not the number the market trades.

**Three supply numbers matter:**

1. **Nominal supply (headline):** ~2.59M XCP
2. **Effective circulating (remove stuck/lost/locked):** ~1.92M XCP
3. **Price-setting float (what can realistically hit bids):** much smaller than either—because most balances don’t behave like sellers.

On a sell-side definition of activity, the float is thin:

- **Sell-side active (&lt;1y, debit-based):** **~196K XCP (7.7%)**
- **Recently acquired or spent (&lt;1y, hybrid):** **~340K XCP (13.3%)**
- **Deep dormant (5y+ on hybrid dormancy):** **~1.50M XCP (58.8%)**

And the tape looks like an accumulation regime: low velocity, low "old supply" disturbance—similar in structure to the quiet setup that preceded XCP's 2020–2021 repricing.

## The mental model: three floats, not one supply

When you look at a token like XCP, you want three different answers:

### 1) “How many exist?”
That’s **nominal supply**. It matters for valuation math and scarcity narrative, but it’s not tradable reality.

### 2) “How many are even available in principle?”
That’s **effective circulating**: nominal supply minus coins that are locked, stuck, or plausibly unrecoverable.

### 3) “How many actually behave like sellers?”
That’s the **price-setting float**: coins held by entities that have recently demonstrated outbound behavior (selling/spending/transferring) and are therefore plausible marginal supply.

Most token analysis collapses these into one number. XCP punishes that mistake.

- **Sleeping** (multi-year “no outbound” behavior)

## 1.5) Prices are set at the margin (float &gt; supply)

There’s an old-school Bitcoin framing that’s still the cleanest way to think about this. Trace Mayer put it bluntly in 2013:

> “Remember, prices are set at the margin… what’s unknown is the float that is available for sale.”

That’s the entire game.

XCP’s protocol supply is knowable. The float is **behavioral**. A coin can exist on-chain and still not be part of the market—because the holder doesn’t sell, can’t sell, or isn’t even around anymore.

Here’s the practical translation:

- **Headline supply** tells you how scarce the asset is in theory.
- **Float** tells you how violent price discovery can be in practice.

When net demand hits a thin tape, price doesn’t “trend” upward. It gaps. It teleports.

**So now let’s measure behavior.**

## 2) Dormancy: most balances are not price-setting liquidity

Even after you remove stuck/lost/locked supply, the remainder still doesn’t behave like a “float.”

A common mistake is defining “active” as **any ledger activity** (credits or debits). That counts:

- Exchange cold-storage receiving and internal wallet shuffles
- Addresses that only receive (no sell-side intent)

For float analysis, the question is simpler:

> **Has this holder demonstrated outbound behavior recently?**

### Hybrid dormancy (corrected)

**Definition:**
- If an address has **ever debited** XCP: dormancy = time since **last debit** (sell-side proxy)
- If an address has **never debited** XCP: dormancy = time since **first credit** (how long it has been held)

This is the cleanest “how long has this balance sat without sell-side behavior” approximation you can compute without true coin-age tracking.

### The float that actually matters

There are two useful “active” numbers, depending on how strict you want to be:

**A) Recently acquired or spent (&lt;1y, hybrid):**
**~340K XCP (13.3%) across 689 addresses**

This includes:
- Addresses that spent/sold recently (debits), and
- Addresses that received recently and haven’t sold yet (new holders)

**B) Sell-side active (&lt;1y, debit-based):**
**~196K XCP (7.7%) across 452 addresses**

This is the cleanest proxy for “how much supply has demonstrated outbound behavior recently.”

A useful way to interpret B is as an **upper bound** on price-setting supply: it’s not saying 196K is “for sale,” it’s saying this is the cohort that has *recently proven* it can and will move coins outbound.

**Key claim:** XCP sits on a **large dormant base** with a **thin sell-side float**. That is a classic recipe for discontinuous moves: a thin marginal market with not much supply willing to meet demand.

**Next question:** does “activity” support the idea that sellers are exhausted—or is the chain just noisy because of exchange plumbing?

## 3) Activity and “velocity”: you need to de-noise exchange plumbing

Volume and “velocity” are often used as a proxy for market participation. But for XCP, raw on-chain transfer volume has a known distortion:

**Exchange deposit plumbing can double-count economic flow.**

The pattern:
`user → deposit address → exchange hot wallet`

If you count both legs as “activity,” you inflate volume without changing real sell pressure or real distribution.

In other words:

> A busy exchange can make the chain look “active” even when coins are just being routed.

### Deposit→sweep inflation (why it matters)

When you strip out identified deposit-address sweeps, “velocity” comes down—and recent years look even quieter.

### Adjusted velocity (ex-deposit sweeps)

| Year | Gross Velocity | Adjusted Velocity |
|------|---------------:|------------------:|
| 2019 | 0.91× | 0.81× |
| 2020 | 0.21× | 0.20× |
| 2023 | 0.40× | 0.38× |
| 2024 | 0.24× | 0.23× |
| 2025 | 0.32× | **0.20×** |

**Interpretation:** 2025 is not “busy distribution.” It’s quiet. And once you remove the plumbing noise, it’s even quieter.

This is not automatically bullish. But in low-float markets, quiet often corresponds to **exhaustion of easy sellers** and a holder base that is simply sitting tight.

**Next question:** quiet can still be distribution if old holders are waking up. That’s what SYD checks.

---

## 4) The whale alarm: Supply-Years Destroyed (SYD)

Velocity can be gamed by churn. The more structural signal is: **are old coins waking up?**

That’s what **SYD (Supply-Years Destroyed)** approximates: a proxy for whether dormant supply is being disturbed.

**Formula:** `SYD = LTDD ÷ (Supply × 365)`
Where LTDD sums `amount × days_since_last_activity` at spend time (address-level “last touch” proxy).

### How to read SYD

| Pattern | What it suggests |
|---------|------------------|
| High velocity + low SYD | Churn/speculation (same coins moving) |
| High velocity + high SYD | Real distribution (older coins waking up) |
| Low velocity + low SYD | Quiet accumulation / low disturbance |

### What SYD is *for* (and what it isn’t)

SYD is not a price oracle. It doesn’t “predict up.”

What it does well is answer:

> **Is the long-dormant base starting to sell into bids?**

That’s the failure mode for any “supply crunch” narrative.

## 5) The 2019–2020 setup: quiet tape, then repricing

Here’s the rhyme that matters:

- 2019 felt dead. Low attention. Low urgency.
- 2020 saw a sharp repricing impulse (including a large wick), despite the market “feeling” quiet for long stretches.
- 2021 closed far above the prior regime, implying the repricing stuck.

Using the yearly XCP/USD series:
- **2019 close:** ~$1.44
- **2020 high:** ~$22.6
- **2021 close:** ~$8.67

The structural claim is not “history repeats.”

The claim is: **when the sell-side float is thin and dormant supply stays dormant, demand doesn’t need to be huge to move price a lot.** Price discovery is a microstructure event, not a smooth walk.

## 6) What would break the thesis

If you want to be intellectually honest about “low float,” name the failure modes.

### 1) Dormant supply wakes up
A supply crunch ends the moment old holders decide “this is my exit.”
**Watch SYD and last-debit shifts.**

### 2) Trapped balances re-enter circulation
Unlikely without a withdrawal path, but not impossible (legal/bankruptcy outcomes).

### 3) New exchange listings expand accessible float
More venues = more liquidity = less convexity. (Also: less “teleporting.”)

### 4) Protocol activity stays low forever
Low volume can mean exhaustion—or irrelevance. The difference is whether Counterparty activity returns.

## Appendix: Methodology

**Data source:** Full Counterparty event history (19.9M events), blocks 278,000–933,605 (Jan 2014–Jan 2025).

**Reconciliation:** Supply reconstructed from ledger entries matches protocol supply within **2 XCP**.

### Dormancy (hybrid)
- If address has ever debited XCP: **last debit date**
- If address has never debited XCP: **first credit date**

### Deposit address adjustment (de-noising volume)
Exchange deposit addresses (user → deposit addr → exchange hot wallet) create double-counted transfer volume.

We identified **9,125 deposit addresses** by pattern-matching:
- low transaction count (≤10),
- near-zero ending balance,
- outbound sends exclusively to known exchange wallets.

Adjusted velocity excludes these deposit→sweep legs from transfer volume. This adjustment reduces measured “activity,” and it is most impactful in years where exchange plumbing is a larger fraction of on-chain transfers.

### SYD
SYD uses an address-level “last-touch” proxy (LTDD). It is not perfect coin-age (true coin-age would require FIFO/LIFO tracking), and it can overstate age for mixed-age balances. The purpose is regime detection: “is old supply waking up?”

Exchange estimates are lower bounds based on known wallets.
